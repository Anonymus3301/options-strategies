Strategy Explained

Construction: Breeden & Litzenberger (1978) showed the risk-neutral probability density of the terminal price equals the second derivative of the call price with respect to strike. With r=0 (this app's convention), f(K) = d²C/dK² exactly. OTM puts below spot are first converted to their call-equivalent price via put-call parity, so the whole curve is one consistent unit before the finite-difference second derivative is taken.

Market view: none by itself — a measurement, not a position. It's the natural extension of the Variance Swap page: that page compresses the whole smile into one number (fair vol); this page recovers the whole shape the smile implies — skewness, fat tails, and where the market's most likely outcome actually sits.

Why it matters: Black-Scholes itself assumes a symmetric lognormal terminal distribution. The green curve here is what the market's actual quoted smile implies instead; the gray reference curve is the lognormal a single ATM IV would imply. Where they diverge is exactly where the market disagrees with the Black-Scholes assumption — visible directly, not just inferred from a skew number.

How to use it: compare the mode (most likely terminal price) against spot, and the green curve's shape against the gray reference — a green curve sitting well below/above the gray one at a given strike says the market prices that outcome as more/less likely than a plain lognormal would.

Key risk / caveat: a second derivative amplifies quote noise, so this needs a reasonably liquid, densely-quoted chain — thin strikes or wide bid/ask spreads (this uses mark price, but marks can still be stale on thin strikes) can produce a bumpy or unreliable curve. Flagged as "insufficient data" below a minimum strike count.

Not financial advice. This is a discretized numerical second derivative of quoted option prices — inherently noisier than a first-derivative quantity like the Variance Swap page's fair vol. Densities are clipped at zero (a real risk-neutral density can't be negative, but finite-difference noise on a thin chain occasionally produces small negative values) and the curve is shown as measured, not smoothed or fitted to any parametric model.
