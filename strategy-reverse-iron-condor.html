Strategy Explained

Construction: buy an inner strangle (call and put close to ATM), sell an outer strangle further out to help fund it — the same 4 legs as an Iron Condor with long/short flipped.

Market view: expects a big move in either direction past the outer strikes — the debit mirror of the Iron Condor's neutral, range-bound thesis.

Why trade it: cheaper than an outright long strangle since the short outer legs help pay for the long inner ones, at the cost of capping the maximum profit.

Max loss: the full net debit paid, occurring anywhere price finishes between the two inner strikes at expiry. Max profit: capped, achieved once price moves beyond either outer strike.

Key risk: the flat loss zone in the middle is the single most likely outcome for most assets most of the time, which is exactly why this needs a real move to pay off; without the short outer legs this would just be a plain long strangle.
