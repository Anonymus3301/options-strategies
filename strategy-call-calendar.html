Strategy Explained

Construction: sell 1 front-month ATM call, buy 1 back-month call at the same strike — a single option leg per expiry, unlike the plain Calendar Spread page's full straddle (call + put) at each expiry.

Market view: mildly bullish-to-neutral with a term-structure lean — wants price to sit at or drift slightly above the strike by front expiry, letting the front leg decay while the back leg holds value.

Why trade it: half the cost and half the legs of the straddle Calendar Spread, appropriate when a trader wants the time-decay edge without also expressing a view on the put side.

Max loss: the net debit paid, if things go badly wrong (typically a large move either direction, or an IV collapse). Max profit: maximized if price sits near the strike at front expiry.

Key risk: the back leg's value at front expiry is estimated via Black-Scholes at its current IV — a simplification, since IV can move by then. Unlike the straddle Calendar, this leans bullish rather than being purely neutral.
