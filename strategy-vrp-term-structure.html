Strategy Explained

Construction: for every live expiry, VRP = ATM IV − realized vol computed over a lookback matched to that expiry's own days-to-expiry (a 7-day expiry compared to 7-day realized vol, a 60-day expiry to 60-day realized vol, and so on) — using the same real daily-close history as the Realized Volatility Cone page, not the fixed 7/14/30-day windows the Premium Selling page compares every expiry against.

Market view: none by itself — a measurement, not a position. It's the maturity-matched combination of two ideas already on this site: the IV Term Structure Curve's per-expiry IV and the Volatility Cone's real historical realized vol.

Why it matters: Premium Selling asks "is IV rich vs. realized in general." This page asks the sharper question a real premium seller actually needs answered: "given I have to pick one expiry, which one is currently the richest to sell (or the cheapest to buy)?" — since front and back months can carry very different premiums relative to their own matched realized vol.

How to use it: the richest-VRP expiry is a candidate for premium-selling structures (see Iron Condor, Premium Selling); the cheapest is a candidate for long-vol structures (see Long Volatility) — cross-reference either with the IV Term Structure Curve to see the raw IV shape alongside this premium read. The front-expiry VRP is also tracked day-over-day in this browser for a Rank/Percentile read, completing a trio with the Skew Arbitrage and Convexity Arb pages' own RR/BF rank tracking — three independent, orthogonal vol-surface signals.

Key risk / caveat: uses a single ATM strike's IV per expiry (not whole-chain model-free vol) and BTC's own realized vol history, which is a limited (~400-day) sample that can shift regime without warning.
