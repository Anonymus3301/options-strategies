Strategy Explained

Construction: no underlying position — just two legs, each near 25-delta. Bullish: buy an OTM call, sell an OTM put. Bearish: buy an OTM put, sell an OTM call. Unlike the Synthetic Forward (same ATM strike, one-to-one payoff everywhere), the two different OTM strikes here leave a flat "dead zone" between them where the position earns or loses only the net premium, nothing more.

Market view: a leveraged, capital-efficient directional bet — same market view as a Bull/Bear Call/Put Spread, but undefined risk on one side instead of a capped debit, in exchange for often being enterable near zero cost or even a credit.

Why trade it: the short leg's premium subsidizes (or fully funds) the long leg, so a directional view can be expressed for much less capital than an outright long call/put — at the cost of real, undefined risk on the short leg if the market moves against it.

Max profit / max loss: unlimited profit on the long leg's side; on the short leg's side, loss grows without limit as price moves further away (floor-bounded only at BTC = 0 for a short put). Breakeven: because both outer slopes point the same direction, this payoff is monotonic — there is exactly one overall breakeven, not two. It sits above the call strike if the structure is a net debit, or below the put strike if it's a net credit (shown below).

Key risk: this is not a defined-risk spread — it has a genuinely naked leg. The "dead zone" can look deceptively similar to a defined-risk collar, but there is no held underlying here providing offsetting protection; the short leg's risk is real and uncapped.
