Strategy Explained

Construction: a call spread (long the lower strike, short the higher strike) plus a put spread (short the lower strike, long the higher strike), at the same two strikes and expiry.

Market view: none — direction-agnostic. The combined payoff at expiry is a fixed amount (the strike width) no matter where price ends up, making this a synthetic financing instrument rather than a market bet.

Why trade it: comparing the fixed payout to what the chain's current prices say the position costs today implies an annualized interest rate — if that rate looks attractive vs. other financing options, this is effectively a way to lend or borrow using options.

Max profit / max loss: neither applies in the usual sense — the payoff is fixed by construction; the only real "risk" is execution risk across four legs.

Key risk: assumes all four legs fill near the quoted marks and the position is held to expiry. Deribit's crypto options are European-style, which removes early-assignment risk that would otherwise complicate this on American-style venues.

Not financial advice, and not risk-free in practice. A box spread's payoff is fixed only in theory — European settlement, no counterparty/margin risk, and the ability to actually trade all four legs at the quoted mark prices are all idealizations. This page reports what the chain's current mark prices imply, not an executable rate.
