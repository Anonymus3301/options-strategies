Strategy Explained

Construction: sell a front-month synthetic forward (short call + long put, same strike K) and buy a back-month synthetic forward (long call + short put, same strike K) — four legs total, two expiries, one strike.

Market view: none directly — like the Box Spread and Synthetic Forward pages, this is a cross-check on pricing consistency, not a directional or volatility bet.

Why it matters: by put-call parity, each synthetic forward's cost reflects that expiry's own implied forward price. Combining a short front synthetic with a long back synthetic at the same strike means the entry cash flow reduces to (front-implied forward) − (back-implied forward) — in a perfectly consistent market this should be close to zero; a nonzero reading reveals the two expiries' chains disagreeing about the forward curve.

Not a payoff — a snapshot: unlike the Box Spread's single fixed payout at one expiry, a Jelly Roll's front leg settles before its back leg does, so it does not collapse to a clean, riskless terminal payoff — once the front leg is gone, the remaining back synthetic is a live, fully directional position again. This page reports the entry cash flow only, not a completed trade.

Key risk / caveat: executing this in practice means carrying real directional exposure in the back leg after the front leg settles — it is a pricing-consistency signal, not a riskless arbitrage capture, despite superficially resembling one.
