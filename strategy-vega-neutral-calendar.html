Strategy Explained

Construction: the same ATM straddle calendar as the plain Calendar Spread page (sell front, buy back, same strike) — but instead of a 1:1 ratio, the back-month quantity is scaled so the position's net vega at inception is zero, computed from each leg's Black-Scholes vega.

Market view: neutral on implied vol itself; the bet is purely on time decay (theta) and realized-move convexity (gamma) over the front expiry's life, with the usual "IV might move and change everything" risk of a plain calendar deliberately hedged away at the start.

Why trade it: a plain 1:1 calendar is a mixed bet — it wins if the front decays faster than the back and loses if IV falls, or vice versa, so its P&L conflates two different views. Neutralizing vega isolates the theta/gamma view a calendar is usually intended to express.

Max profit / max loss: not a single fixed number — depends on the realized path, same as the plain Calendar Spread; see the payoff chart.

Key risk: "vega-neutral" holds only at the moment it's computed — vega itself changes as price and time pass (this is a static hedge, not continuously rebalanced), and the exact ratio may require a fractional contract size to implement precisely.

The ratio shown is a snapshot computed from today's quoted IVs and today's Black-Scholes vega — it is not rebalanced as the position ages, and real fills at a fractional contract size may not be available on every venue. This does not neutralize gamma or theta, only vega — the position still has real, intentional exposure to both.
