Built entirely from calls: buy the outer wings, sell the inner body — a wider, flatter-topped cousin of the Butterfly Spread

Strategy Explained

Construction: buy 1 call at K1 (lowest), sell 1 call at K2, sell 1 call at K3, buy 1 call at K4 (K1 < K2 < K3 < K4), all calls, same expiry — four strikes instead of the Butterfly's three.

Market view: neutral — a bet that price finishes somewhere between K2 and K3, with more tolerance for being "close but not exact" than a Butterfly's single-point peak.

Why trade it: spreading the short strikes apart (K2 to K3) instead of stacking them at one strike trades some of the Butterfly's peak profit for a wider flat-top zone where the max profit is still achieved — a defined-risk bet on a range rather than a point.

Max profit: flat across the whole body (between K2 and K3), equal to (K2 − K1) − net debit. Max loss: the net debit paid, identical on both tails (below K1 and above K4) when the wings are equal width, as they are here. Breakevens: K1 + net debit, and K4 − net debit.

Key risk: the wider profit zone costs more than a Butterfly's tighter one — check the actual debit against the payoff before assuming "wider is strictly better."

Not financial advice. Strike selection rounds to the nearest listed strike on each leg, so the position may not be perfectly symmetric even when the selected widths are equal. Verified that the two flat tail regions match exactly when wing widths are equal, and that a put condor at the same 4 strikes produces an identical payoff — both confirmed numerically before shipping this page.
