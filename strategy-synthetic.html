Strategy Explained

Construction: long a call plus short a put, both at the ATM strike and same expiry — this replicates a long forward position on the underlying.

Market view: directionally long BTC, exactly like owning a forward contract at that strike — this is not a volatility play.

Why trade it: compares the options-implied forward price from this synthetic construction against the actual dated future's mark price for the same expiry, a cross-check for mispricing between the two markets.

Max profit / max loss: same as owning the underlying outright from the strike — unlimited upside, large downside bounded only by BTC reaching zero.

Key risk: this is a fully directional position dressed as an arbitrage-style comparison; acting on a discrepancy found here means taking on the same directional exposure as holding a forward, not a risk-free trade.

Not financial advice, and not risk-free in practice. A real conversion/reversal also needs a matching-expiry dated future to actually exist (not every options expiry has one), and executing all three legs (call, put, future) simultaneously at quoted mark prices is an idealization — real fills, fees, and margin all eat into any apparent edge. This page reports what current mark prices imply, not an executable arbitrage.
