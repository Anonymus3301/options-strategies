Strategy Explained

Construction: two calendar spreads run side by side — sell a front-month OTM call and buy a back-month call at the same (higher) strike, plus sell a front-month OTM put and buy a back-month put at the same (lower) strike. Four legs, two expiries, two strikes.

Market view: neutral, like the single ATM Calendar Spread, but betting price stays inside a range between the two strikes rather than pinning to one exact point.

Why trade it: a single ATM calendar's profit zone is narrow and peaks at one strike; spreading the two calendars out to OTM strikes on both sides trades some of that peak profit for a much wider zone where the position still works, at the cost of paying for two calendars instead of one.

Max loss: the total net debit paid, if price makes a big enough move that both front legs finish deep ITM before their back-month replacements can offset it. Max profit: not a single clean number — it depends on where price sits relative to both strikes at front expiry; see the payoff chart.

Key risk: both back legs' remaining value at front expiry are estimated via Black-Scholes at their current IV — a simplification, since IV can move by then — and this costs more upfront than a single calendar for a wider (not unlimited) range of safety.
