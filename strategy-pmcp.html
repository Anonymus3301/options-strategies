Strategy Explained

Construction: buy a deep in-the-money put at the furthest available expiry (a high-delta put stands in for a short BTC position), then sell a near-dated out-of-the-money put against it — the bearish mirror of the Poor Man's Covered Call.

Market view: bearish-to-neutral, same as a real covered put — willing to cap profit below the short strike in exchange for income, wanting the long leg to track BTC's decline closely.

Why trade it: the deep ITM put costs a fraction of spot instead of the full margin/capital a real short position ties up, while still moving roughly delta-for-delta with the underlying. Selling the near-dated put collects income against it.

Max profit: reached exactly at the short strike at front expiry (the long leg still has some remaining time value there, so it's usually a bit more than the naive "strike width minus debit" estimate). Max loss: capped at the net debit paid — a real advantage over an actual short position, whose downside is unlimited if price rises.

Key risk: the long put's remaining value at front expiry is estimated via Black-Scholes at its current IV — a simplification, since IV can move by then — and the long put isn't a perfect substitute for a short position: it still has time value and a delta below 1.0 in magnitude, so it won't track price exactly.

Not financial advice. "Far expiry" here means the furthest expiry Deribit currently lists, not a multi-year LEAPS contract — crypto options don't trade that far out. Capital efficiency is real, but the long put still expires eventually and needs to be rolled, unlike an actual short position.
