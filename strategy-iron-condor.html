Strategy Explained

Construction: sell a call and a put around 20Δ each, then buy further-out call and put wings for protection — 4 legs total.

Market view: neutral — the same range-bound thesis as the Premium Selling page's short strangle, but with the tail risk capped.

Why trade it: collects premium from a range-bound bet while capping the worst case, at the cost of giving up some of the credit a naked short strangle would collect.

Max profit: the net credit, kept if price finishes between the two short strikes. Max loss: capped at (wing width − net credit), whichever wing is breached. Breakeven: short put strike minus net credit, and short call strike plus net credit.

Key risk: max profit only happens if price stays inside a fairly narrow band; a big move still loses, just by a known, bounded amount instead of an unlimited one.

Defined risk here means max loss is capped at expiry (wing width minus credit received) rather than theoretically unbounded like a naked strangle — but that cap can still be a meaningful loss, and this page doesn't know your account size or risk tolerance. Short strikes are chosen via Black-Scholes delta from each strike's own quoted IV, not live greeks.
