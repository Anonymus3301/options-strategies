Strategy Explained

Construction: for holders — buy a put at K2 (the main floor), sell a further-OTM put at K1 < K2 (cheapens the floor, accepting only partial protection below K1), and sell a call at K3 (finances the rest, capping upside) — three legs plus the held position, vs. the Collar's two.

Market view: neutral-to-mildly-bullish, like a Collar, but willing to accept a secondary, weaker floor in exchange for a cheaper (or fully financed) primary one.

Why trade it: a plain Collar's put can be expensive if the floor is close to spot; selling a further-out put funds it further, at the cost of no longer being fully protected in a crash scenario below that second strike — a common trade-off in FX and commodity hedging books when "some protection, cheaply" beats "full protection, expensively."

Max profit: capped at (K3 − spot) + net credit (or minus net debit), above K3. Max loss: below K1, losses resume dollar-for-dollar with the held position (minus the flat protected band's value) — this is not full downside protection, unlike a plain Collar or Protective Put.

Key risk: the point of this structure is precisely that protection is not unlimited below K1 — sized wrong, a Seagull can leave a holder exposed in exactly the crash scenario a hedge is meant to cover.
