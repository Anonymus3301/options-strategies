Strategy Explained

Construction: a variance swap's fair strike can be "replicated" by a static portfolio of out-of-the-money options across every listed strike, each weighted by 1/K² (the "log contract" — the same construction behind the CBOE VIX and Deribit's own DVOL index). This page computes that fair strike from the live chain and reports it as a volatility percentage.

Market view: none by itself — this is a measurement tool, not a position. It's a more rigorous, whole-curve alternative to reading a single ATM IV number when judging whether volatility is "rich" or "cheap."

Why it matters: a single ATM IV ignores the skew and smile entirely. The model-free fair vol aggregates every strike's price, so it moves when skew steepens even if ATM IV itself hasn't — a subtler read on how richly volatility is priced across the whole distribution, not just the center of it.

How to use it: compare fair vol against realized vol the same way the Premium Selling and Long Volatility pages compare ATM IV against realized vol — a large positive gap favors selling volatility broadly (not just ATM), a large negative gap favors buying it.

Key risk / caveat: this is a discretized approximation using whatever strikes are actually listed and quoted — thin chains (few strikes, gaps in quotes) make it less accurate, and it says nothing about which specific strikes or structure to trade.
