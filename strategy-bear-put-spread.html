Strategy Explained

Construction: buy 1 near-ATM put, sell 1 put at a further OTM strike, same expiry — the most basic defined-risk bearish spread.

Market view: moderately bearish — expects price to fall, but not necessarily to zero; happy to cap the payout in exchange for a lower cost than an outright long put.

Why trade it: the short put's premium partially finances the long put, lowering the breakeven and the capital at risk compared to buying the put alone — the tradeoff is giving up any further gains below the short strike.

Max profit: (long strike − short strike) − net debit, at or below the short strike. Max loss: the net debit paid, at or above the long strike. Breakeven: long strike − net debit.

Key risk: if price doesn't fall at all, the full debit is lost — same risk direction as a naked put, just smaller in size on both the cost and the payout.
