Strategy Explained

Construction: the same front-month ATM IV recorded daily in this browser's localStorage that powers the IV Rank feature elsewhere on this site — treated here as a time series in its own right, and run through the same annualized-volatility formula (`qAnnualizedVol`) every realized-vol reading on this site already uses on price closes, just applied to IV levels instead of price levels.

Market view: none by itself — a measurement, not a position. IV Rank asks "is current IV high or low vs. its own recent range." This asks a different question: "how jumpy has IV itself been recently," independent of whether it's currently high or low.

Why it matters: a calendar spread, vega-neutral calendar, or forward-variance position is a bet expressed through vega — and vega's own P&L sensitivity compounds with how much IV itself actually moves, not just its current level. High vol-of-vol means a vega position's mark-to-market can swing hard even with price pinned flat; low vol-of-vol means IV itself is unusually stable right now.

How to use it: cross-reference with the Vega-Neutral Calendar, Forward Variance, and Calendar Spread pages before sizing a vega-heavy position — a high vol-of-vol reading argues for smaller size or a shorter holding period on any of those structures.

Key risk / caveat: this-browser-only history (same limitation as every other Rank feature here), starts from zero on a fresh browser or after clearing site data, and a single front-month ATM IV point per day is a coarse proxy for the whole vol surface's actual day-to-day movement.
