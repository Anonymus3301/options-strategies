Strategy Explained

Construction: the same 4 legs as the Iron Condor Builder (short ~20Δ put and call, long wings for protection), but the two wing widths are set independently instead of sharing one selector — a narrower wing on one side costs more but caps that side's loss tighter; a wider wing on the other costs less but leaves more risk exposed there.

Market view: neutral, like a regular Iron Condor, but with a deliberate lean — a trader more worried about a crash than a rally (or vice versa) can size the wings to match, rather than treating both tails identically.

Why trade it: a symmetric Iron Condor treats upside and downside risk as equally likely and equally sized, which is rarely anyone's actual view. Sizing wings independently lets the credit collected and the risk taken reflect an actual directional lean, while still collecting premium from both sides.

Max profit: the net credit, kept if price finishes between the two short strikes — same as a regular Iron Condor. Max loss: reported separately for the put side and call side, since with unequal wing widths these are no longer the same number.

Key risk: the side with the wider wing carries more uncapped-feeling risk (though still technically capped) — this page reports both max-loss figures honestly rather than quoting only the smaller, more flattering one.
