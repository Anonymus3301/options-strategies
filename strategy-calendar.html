Strategy Explained

Construction: sell the front-month ATM straddle, buy the back-month ATM straddle at the same strike (a "time spread").

Market view: expects the underlying to stay relatively close to the strike through the front expiry, and/or for the front month's IV to fall faster than the back month's.

Why trade it: profits from the front leg decaying faster than the back leg (the normal case since it has less time value to begin with), and/or from an elevated front-month IV normalizing relative to the back month.

Max loss: the net debit paid. Max profit: maximized if price sits near the strike at front expiry, letting the front leg expire worthless while the back leg retains most of its value.

Key risk: the back leg's remaining value at front expiry is estimated via Black-Scholes at its current IV — a simplification, since IV can move by then; a big move away from the strike, or an across-the-board IV drop, hurts both legs at once.
