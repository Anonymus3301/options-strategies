Strategy Explained

Construction: ATM IV (average of the closest-to-spot call and put's quoted IV) computed independently for every live expiry, plotted against each one's days-to-expiry — the whole term structure curve in one view.

Market view: none by itself — a measurement, not a position. It complements the Forward Variance page (which bootstraps the implied vol for the period between exactly two chosen expiries) by showing the overall curve shape across every expiry at once.

Why it matters: the curve's slope is a standard vol-trading read. Upward-sloping (contango) is the typical shape — more time priced with more cumulative uncertainty. Downward-sloping (backwardation) — front-dated IV trading above back-dated — often signals near-term event or stress-driven demand for short-dated protection.

How to use it: a steeply backwardated curve is the classic setup search for calendar-spread candidates (sell the rich front, buy the cheaper back) — cross-reference with the Calendar Spread and Forward Variance pages for the actual trade construction; this page is the whole-curve view that motivates looking.

Key risk / caveat: uses only the single ATM strike's IV per expiry (not a whole-chain model-free number like the Variance Swap page), and needs at least 2 live expiries with a quoted ATM strike to draw a curve at all.
