Strategy Explained

Construction: buy a back-month put at a higher (near-ATM) strike, sell a front-month put at a lower strike — the bearish mirror of the Diagonal Spread page's bullish call construction.

Market view: bearish with a term-structure lean — wants price to drift down toward the short strike by front expiry while the back leg retains value.

Why trade it: combines the Calendar Spread's time-decay edge with bearish directional exposure, typically cheaper than an outright bear put spread since the short leg sits in a nearer expiry.

Max loss: the net debit paid. Max profit: maximized if price finishes right at or near the short strike at front expiry, letting the front leg decay maximally while the back leg keeps most of its value.

Key risk: the back leg's value at front expiry is estimated via Black-Scholes at its current IV — a simplification, since IV can move by then — and no early-assignment or margin costs are modeled.
