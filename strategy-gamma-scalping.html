Strategy Explained

Construction: buy an ATM straddle, then re-hedge its delta with the underlying once a day, buying or selling just enough to stay flat. As price moves, the straddle's delta drifts away from zero (gamma), so each rehedge locks in a small trade — sell into strength, buy into weakness — regardless of which way price ultimately goes.

Market view: direction-agnostic, like the Long Volatility page's straddle — but here the bet isn't "buy and hold," it's "realized volatility (captured via daily rehedging) will exceed the option's time decay."

Why trade it: a delta-hedged long option position isolates a pure volatility bet: the rehedging trades accumulate gains proportional to gamma times the square of each day's move, while theta bleeds a roughly steady cost each day. If realized volatility runs hot enough, the hedge gains outrun the decay.

Max profit / max loss: not fixed the way a static structure's is — both hedge P&L and option decay accumulate day by day over the window, so the outcome here is a backtested result over one specific historical path, not a forward-looking payoff diagram.

Key risk: this needs active, disciplined rehedging (with real trading costs each time) to actually realize the gamma P&L shown — skip a day in a fast market and the whole thesis can break down.

Not financial advice — and not a live simulation. This uses today's ATM IV as a constant assumed pricing/hedging volatility across the entire backtest window, because no historical implied-vol time series is available from this free API — it isolates the effect of the realized price path while holding that vol assumption fixed, which is a simplification. Rehedging is discretized to once per day, and no trading fees, slippage, funding, or margin costs on the hedge are modeled. The strike used is the nearest $500 level to the price at the start of the window, not a strike actually listed on Deribit that far back.
