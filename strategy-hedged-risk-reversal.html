Strategy Explained

Construction: the exact same 25Δ risk reversal as the Skew Arbitrage page (sell the richer 25Δ option, buy the cheaper one), plus a position in the underlying sized to exactly offset the combined position's net delta at inception — computed the same way a market maker would size a hedge for a skew trade.

Market view: the same skew-normalization view as the plain risk reversal, but with the incidental directional lean the Skew Arbitrage page explicitly flags (selling the put/buying the call leans bullish, and vice versa) removed at entry.

Why it matters: a plain risk reversal is a leveraged directional bet with a skew-driven entry cost, not a pure skew trade — this page shows exactly how much flatter (or not) the position's immediate mark-to-market sensitivity becomes once that initial delta is hedged out.

How to read the chart: for small hypothetical moves, the hedged line should sit much closer to zero than the unhedged line — that's the delta hedge doing its job. For large moves, both lines diverge from zero again, since a one-time delta hedge only cancels the linear (first-order) sensitivity, not gamma.

Key risk / caveat: this hedge is sized once, at inception, from today's snapshot — it is not rebalanced as spot moves or time passes. By the time the position reaches expiry, it has long since reacquired real directional exposure unless actively rehedged, the same caveat as the Gamma Scalping and Vega-Neutral Calendar pages.
