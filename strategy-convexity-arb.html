Strategy Explained

Construction: the 25Δ Butterfly (BF25) is the average of the 25Δ call and put IVs minus the ATM IV — a standard FX-desk measure of smile convexity/steepness, independent of which side (calls or puts) carries the skew. This page tracks BF25 against its own recent range and suggests a structure at the extremes: sell the 25Δ strangle + buy the ATM straddle when wings are rich, or the reverse when they're cheap.

Market view: a bet that wing richness itself (not directional skew) is stretched and will normalize — orthogonal to the Skew Arbitrage page's risk-reversal signal. A chain can have a large RR25 (strong directional skew) with a small BF25 (flat convexity), or vice versa; they measure different things.

Why trade it: when BF25 sits at an extreme percentile of its own recent range, the "cheap convexity, buy it" / "rich convexity, sell it" trade profits if wing pricing normalizes toward its typical level relative to the ATM straddle.

Max profit / max loss: the "buy cheap convexity" structure (long wings, short ATM straddle) is exactly the existing Iron Butterfly page's shape — defined risk, entered here on the BF signal instead of a fixed wing width. The "sell rich convexity" structure (short wings, long ATM straddle) is its mirror image and is also defined-risk, since every leg is matched by an opposing one at the same or a different strike.

Key risk: like the Skew Arbitrage page, "rich" or "cheap" is relative to this browser's own short accumulated history, not a long-run historical norm — a stretched reading can stay stretched, or become more stretched, for a long time.
