Strategy Explained

Construction: no options position — this bootstraps the "forward variance" between two expiries from their own model-free implied variances (see the Variance Swap page), the same way a forward interest rate is bootstrapped from two zero rates: Forward Var = (T₂·Var₂ − T₁·Var₁) / (T₂ − T₁).

Market view: none by itself — a measurement, not a position. It answers "what volatility is actually priced in for the period after the front expiry and before the back expiry," which single-point IV numbers at each expiry don't directly tell you.

Why it matters: a Calendar Spread's real economic bet is on this forward-period vol vs. what actually realizes in that window — comparing front IV to back IV directly, as the plain Calendar Spread page does, only shows the two endpoints, not the rate priced in between them.

How to use it: if forward vol looks rich vs. your own realized-vol expectation for that future window, a calendar-type structure (selling the front, buying the back) is set up to profit from it normalizing lower — and vice versa if it looks cheap.

Key risk / caveat: inherits the Variance Swap page's discretization approximation twice over (once per expiry), and a thin chain at either expiry can make the bootstrapped number unstable — flagged as "insufficient data" when either side doesn't have enough listed strikes.

Not financial advice. Forward variance can come out negative (an arithmetic impossibility for real variance) when the two expiries' fair vols are inconsistent with each other — usually a sign of thin or stale quotes at one of them rather than a genuine market signal. This page reports "insufficient data" rather than showing a nonsensical negative volatility in that case.
