Strategy Explained

Construction: for one selected expiry, plots each strike's quoted OTM IV (puts below spot, calls above — the same convention as the Variance Swap page's OTM price curve) against its strike, with the ATM, 25Δ put, and 25Δ call points marked.

Market view: none by itself — a measurement, not a position. It's the visual, whole-curve counterpart to the Skew Arbitrage and Convexity Arb pages' single RR25/BF25 numbers — this is literally the curve those two numbers are extracted from.

Why it matters: RR25 (the IV gap between the two marked 25Δ points) and BF25 (their average vs. the ATM point) are useful summaries, but a summary can hide shape — a smile can be lopsided, kinked, or have a flat side that a single number wouldn't reveal. Seeing the actual curve shows whether the summary numbers are describing a clean, textbook smile or something stranger.

How to use it: cross-reference with Skew Arbitrage and Convexity Arb — if their rank/percentile readings say RR25 or BF25 is "stretched," look at the shape here to see whether that stretch is a clean, tradeable skew/convexity read or an artifact of one noisy strike.

Key risk / caveat: needs at least a handful of quoted strikes on both sides of spot for this expiry; thin chains will show as "insufficient data." Uses each strike's own quoted mark IV, not a smoothed or fitted curve.
