Strategy Explained

Construction: sell a front-month OTM call and OTM put (closer to spot), buy a back-month OTM call and OTM put at wider strikes (further from spot) — a call diagonal and a put diagonal run side by side, four legs and two expiries.

Market view: neutral, wanting price to stay inside a range through front expiry — the same thesis as the Double Calendar, but the back legs sitting further out (rather than at the same strikes as the front, like the Double Calendar) usually makes this cheaper to put on for a similar-sized range.

Why trade it: selling closer-to-the-money front options collects more premium than the Double Calendar's identical-strike construction, while the wider back legs still cap the risk if front expiry arrives with price outside the range.

Max loss: the net debit (or credit, if collected upfront) — see the actual net cost figure, since whether this is a net payer or receiver of premium depends on the specific strikes and IV. Max profit: not a single clean number — depends on where price sits relative to all four strikes at front expiry; see the payoff chart.

Key risk: both back legs' remaining value at front expiry are estimated via Black-Scholes at their current IV — a simplification shared with every calendar/diagonal page on this site.
