Strategy Explained

Construction: no position here — this computes the "max pain" strike per expiry, the strike at which total option-holder payout, summed across all open interest, is minimized.

Market view: a theory that price tends to gravitate toward max pain as expiry approaches, on the idea that option writers (often larger, better-capitalized players) have some influence on where price settles.

Why shown: as a data point some traders watch for potential pinning near expiry — not a trade recommendation by itself, and no position is suggested or scored on this page.

Max profit / max loss: not applicable — there is no constructed position here to score.

Key risk / caveat: max pain is explicitly a contested theory with weak empirical support; treat it as one input among many, not a forecast of where price will actually land.

Not financial advice — and contested even among practitioners. "Max pain" theory claims price gravitates toward the strike where option buyers collectively lose the most (writers lose the least) as expiry nears, the idea being that option writers have an incentive/ability to hedge price toward that level. Empirical support for this is weak and disputed; plenty of expiries settle nowhere near their max pain strike. This page reports the computed max pain level from open interest as data, not as a forecast.
