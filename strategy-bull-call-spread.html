Strategy Explained

Construction: buy 1 near-ATM call, sell 1 call at a further OTM strike, same expiry — the most basic defined-risk directional spread.

Market view: moderately bullish — expects price to rise, but not necessarily explosively; happy to cap the upside in exchange for a lower cost than an outright long call.

Why trade it: the short call's premium partially finances the long call, lowering the breakeven and the capital at risk compared to buying the call alone — the tradeoff is giving up any gains above the short strike.

Max profit: (short strike − long strike) − net debit, at or above the short strike. Max loss: the net debit paid, at or below the long strike. Breakeven: long strike + net debit.

Key risk: if price doesn't rise at all, the full debit is lost — same risk direction as a naked call, just smaller in size on both the cost and the payout.
